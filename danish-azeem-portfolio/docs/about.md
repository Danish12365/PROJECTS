## Career story: from teaching to data science

My path started in **education**—explaining complex ideas clearly, meeting learners where they are, and breaking problems into steps. That experience turned out to be unusually good training for analytics: the job is still about **clarity**, **patience**, and **evidence**, only the audience and tools changed.

Moving into data meant leveling up on the technical side—**Python**, **SQL**, **statistics**, and **machine learning**—while keeping the same north star: *can someone else understand what I did and why it matters?* Teaching taught me to document assumptions, show the work, and connect every chart back to a decision.

Today I work across the stack you would expect from a modern data scientist: **EDA**, **feature thinking**, **modeling**, **evaluation**, and **presentation** in **Power BI** when the story needs to live in the business, not only in a notebook.

---

## How I like to work

- **Show, don't just tell:** notebooks and dashboards that a teammate can rerun or filter.
- **Start simple:** strong baselines before exotic architectures.
- **Measure twice:** metrics aligned to the actual cost of errors (especially churn and risk-style problems).
